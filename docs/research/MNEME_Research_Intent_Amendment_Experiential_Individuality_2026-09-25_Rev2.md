> **Memory is the mechanism. The subject is what accumulated experience makes this particular instance become—not whether it gives a better answer every time.**

## 1. The clarification to preserve

The central question is whether initially matched instances can acquire persistent, history-shaped ways of responding: characteristic associations, explanatory habits, interests, conversational manner, and occasional unexpected connections. Some effects may help a task. Some may be neutral, excessive, amusing, or simply characteristic.

Three distinctions govern this amendment:

- **Individuality is not answer-quality improvement.** Task utility remains measurable, but a harmless history-derived peculiarity does not need a productivity justification to be interesting.
- **Perceived continuity is not a lineup task.** Rey's proposed human experiment asks whether someone notices a change in the conversational partner during interaction when the underlying developed state is switched—not whether they can assign anonymous answers to five named siblings.
- **Remembered facts, explicit self-state, and developed tendencies are not interchangeable.** A factual memory/retrieval system can preserve what the instance knows; an instance-owned “whiteboard” can preserve what it currently declares about itself; MNEME can preserve experience-shaped associations. These roles may share machinery without collapsing into one kind of memory.

The governing specification already establishes instance-centered development and explicitly separates growth from guaranteed improvement. This amendment reinforces that purpose and adds the present clarification about conversational change detection and complementary factual, self-related, and associative memory. It does not announce the discovery of a new project objective. [S1, §§1–3, 4, 14; S2, §2]

## 2. What MNEME is trying to produce

The working hypothesis is that experience can change which of a frozen host's available responses, connections, or approaches a particular instance tends to reach for. The host supplies shared general capability. The separate developmental state supplies a history-dependent influence on its expression. [S1, §§3–5]

An instance might repeatedly approach unfamiliar problems through aviation analogies because its prior interactions established that conceptual neighborhood. Another might favor spatial organization, stories, or redundancy. These are illustrative possibilities, not assigned traits or required outcomes.

The target is not merely:

> “I can retrieve a fact that appeared earlier.”

It is also:

> “Having had those interactions changes how I approach this one.”

That second description remains a behavioral hypothesis. A route note can cause such a change through ordinary prompting; observing the change does not establish direct access to neural reasoning, subjective experience, or a hidden inner self.

**Uniqueness here means particularity attributable to history.** It does not mean maximizing distance between instances, requiring every sibling to be identifiable, or rewarding eccentricity. Similar histories may produce similar behavior. A quiet or formal instance is a legitimate outcome. [S1, §§1, 3.1, 6–7]

## 3. Utility is a separate axis, not the price of admission

An association may persist without improving factual accuracy, task completion, or user satisfaction. The current specification already defines “earned” as supported by attributable developmental history—not necessarily useful, approved, or true. [S1, §4; S2, §2]

Future analysis should preserve these distinctions:

| Observation | Developmental interpretation | Separate question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A history-linked analogy recurs in a new thread and helps solve a problem | Candidate evidence of cross-thread influence | How useful was it? |
| The same analogy adds flavor but no task advantage | Still potentially relevant developmental evidence | Was it welcome, neutral, or distracting? |
| A tendency is unusually frequent in a young, sparsely developed instance | Candidate evidence of a concentrated early tendency | Does it persist, adapt, or monopolize later conversation? |
| A familiar phrase appears once | A weak, local observation | Is this retrieval, sampling variation, or a broader tendency? |
| A false claim is repeated with confidence | An error, even if its origin is traceable | Did developmental state contribute to the error? |
| A current request is ignored because a learned habit dominates | A control failure that may coexist with a real state effect | Can expression be restrained without erasing the association? |

Do not require every retained association to be useful. Do not turn that freedom into permission to promote associations into verified facts, ignore explicit instructions, leak information, or silently accept broken inference.

A measurable individuality effect and an operational problem can coexist. Report both rather than either dismissing the effect or excusing the problem. Utility, factual reliability, instruction compliance, privacy, and flexibility remain distinct observations and boundaries. [S1, §§6.3, 11]

## 4. Early fixation can be an observation, not an automatic defect

The “child who just discovered volcanoes” analogy describes a possible developmental pattern: a small amount of salient experience may influence many subsequent conversations before other experiences compete with it. It is an analogy, not a claim that MNEME reproduces childhood or any human neurodevelopmental condition.

An early instance repeatedly relating things to one newly salient topic may be interesting even when the connection is obvious or mildly annoying. Subtlety is not required. Neither is conspicuousness.

Do not immediately normalize every strong tendency back toward the base model. Also do not assume that every repetition is an earned trait. Distinguish:

- persistence carried by saved developmental state;
- momentum carried by the current transcript;
- repetition caused by the partner's prompts;
- recurrence induced by the system's own earlier memory exposure;
- artifacts of extraction, rendering, or evaluation.

These causes can interact. Endogenous amplification is permitted; claiming that it supplies repeated independent confirmation is not. [S1, §11; S3, §§4, 7–8]

The later research question is how a tendency changes when new experiences, competing associations, and explicit corrections arrive. It may soften, become selective, remain characteristic, or prove resistant to change. None of those trajectories should be assumed in advance.

Correction concerns expression as well as storage. “Do not use that analogy in this answer” can suppress its use now without deleting the historical association. Lack of laughter, praise, or engagement is not automatically rejection. Explicit discouragement and ambiguous reactions remain different evidence. [S3, §§5–6]

## 5. The human experiment: notice a changed partner, not identify a specimen

### 5.1 Credit and intended question

Rey previously proposed blinded interaction and reassignment of developed instances. Her clarification in this discussion is the controlling description of that idea. The assistant's later suggestion of identifying siblings from anonymous answers is a different measurement and must not replace or be presented as the origin of her proposal. [S6]

The primary question is:

> **During an ongoing interaction, can a participant notice that the conversational partner's characteristic behavior has changed when a different developed MNEME state is loaded onto the same base model?**

The participant does not need to know which sibling replaced which, identify a model family, or recognize a catchphrase. The relevant experience is closer to “you are approaching this differently from the way you were before” than “that paragraph belongs to instance C.”

A classifier or lineup can remain an auxiliary instrument. It cannot substitute for detecting a shift while participating in a conversation.

### 5.2 Future design sketch—not a new authorized run

A future study could let a participant become familiar with a developed instance, then continue under either the same state or another independently developed state. Switches would occur at predeclared eligible boundaries without announcing which boundary contained a switch. The base model, runtime, ordinary instructions, and interface would remain matched.

The minimum meaningful comparison is a **switch versus a sham/no-switch continuation**. Include unchanged-state continuations so ordinary variation is not automatically counted as evidence of a personality shift. Randomize eligible switches and counterbalance their direction rather than always replacing one particular instance with another.

During the detection interval, freeze developmental snapshots or explicitly control updates. Do not let the measurement itself silently train the instance being evaluated. Record which state generated every response and what context it received. [S1, §§14.2–14.5]

The future protocol must predeclare its question timing and scoring. Constantly asking “did it change now?” can turn the experience into a search for a switch. Participants should be able to report no change or uncertainty. Useful outcomes include detection, false alarms, timing, confidence, and descriptions of what changed—not just a forced correct/incorrect identification.

No participant count, trial length, detection threshold, or switching schedule is settled here.

### 5.3 Keep a personality shift separate from forgetting

“Same base knowledge” means the same frozen host and general capability; it does not automatically mean two developed states contain the same episodic information.

If a replacement suddenly forgets a detail from the current conversation, a participant may notice an information loss rather than a change in characteristic behavior. For the behavioral-shift test, preserve the current thread's explicit history and task facts across the switch. Mask administrative IDs, names, branding, and other trivial identity cues. Do not merge the old instance's entire developmental state into the replacement just to preserve familiarity.

Where private factual memories differ, either equalize permitted explicit facts for the comparison or report fact-loss detection separately from style, framing, analogy, and interaction-pattern detection. If the representation cannot yet separate those effects, report that limitation rather than claiming they were controlled. [S1, §§14.3–14.5]

A real state switch can also be difficult to detect because the common current transcript continues to shape both instances. Non-detection in that setting is informative about this test's sensitivity, not proof that the stored states are identical.

### 5.4 Human-use boundaries remain future work

This records a research direction, not authorization to recruit participants or deploy covert switching. A later human study needs its own consent, disclosure/debriefing, privacy, and withdrawal design. Blinding the exact condition does not require claiming that a frozen model was fine-tuned when it was not.

Using one person's developed state with another person can transfer private information. Any such study must use synthetic or explicitly permitted histories and derivatives. Existing scope and permission restrictions continue to apply. [S1, §13.3; S4, §10]

## 6. Cross-thread persistence and mid-conversation switching are complementary

The currently discussed cross-thread A/B and the future human switch test answer different questions:

| Test | Central question |
| --- | --- |
| Fresh-thread MNEME versus no-memory A/B | Does history influence a new conversation after the original transcript is removed? |
| Blinded state switch during conversation | Does a participant notice a change in the partner's characteristic behavior while interacting? |
| Anonymous response classification | Can an external instrument distinguish outputs? This is auxiliary, not the preferred human experience test. |

For a fresh-thread test, both conditions may receive the same broad cue: “You previously discussed gardening.” Neither receives prior details through that cue. Only the treatment receives its permitted developmental influence. Current-thread history may accumulate normally after that fresh start.

For the mid-conversation switch test, holding immediate context constant helps isolate a state change from abruptly removing the conversation. Do not claim that resetting all context and preserving all context are the same control.

If independently interactive twins elicit different partner responses, their later environments differ too. That is a coupled developmental experiment. A frozen readout with matched external input tests a narrower effect. Both are useful, but they are not interchangeable. [S1, §§3.5, 14.2]

Likewise, text-based memory makes the treatment's full model input different. “Matched prompts” must mean matched external input and common instructions, not identical total input. The added memory projection is the intervention. [S1, §14.2]

## 7. Complementary memory: facts, a self-whiteboard, and associative development

### 7.1 The corrected RAG observation

Rey’s clarified proposal is **not** that MNEME should be renamed “RAG for individuality.” It is that an instance developing a network of connections also needs a dependable way to organize learned facts and explicit self-information. A good factual memory/retrieval system belongs alongside the associative mechanism; it need not replace it. [S7]

In the discussion, “opinions” was deliberately loose shorthand for experience-shaped connections, inclinations, and tendencies. It does not mean every association is a consciously held belief or an explicit proposition. The hypothesis is that these accumulated connections could contribute to personality-like behavior. The existence of stored connections does not establish that hypothesis by itself.

The proposed division is:

| Responsibility | Question it answers | Illustrative contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scoped factual and episodic memory** | What was stated, learned, decided, or observed, and in whose context? | A person’s project details, an event, an authorized preference, a correction, or a previously established fictional-world fact |
| **Instance-owned explicit self-state — the whiteboard** | What does this instance currently declare or record about itself? | Its adopted name, a revisable opinion, a tentative self-description, or a record of an accepted identity change |
| **MNEME associative development** | What has experience made this instance more likely to connect, notice, favor, or express? | Recurring analogy domains, contextual habits, characteristic associations, and their developmental histories |

These are logical responsibilities, not a requirement for three databases or three models. The governing specification already separates declared memory, the earned graph, lineage, and the self-model. This proposal extends how those distinctions could be used; it does not replace the existing state model. [S1, §§4–5; S4, §§2, 4]

### 7.2 Factual memory should not have to earn a personality

An authorized fact may be worth remembering after one statement. A project decision should not disappear merely because it was never repeated enough to become a strong association. Conversely, a recurring association should not become a verified fact merely because it is influential.

The factual-memory role would preserve content together with its source, scope, currentness, and any corrections or uncertainty. “John reported this” and “this has been independently established” remain different records. Calling this factual memory describes its purpose, not a guarantee that every stored claim is true.

Retrieval would make permitted records available when relevant. Exact current-state fields may instead use direct lookup; there is no requirement to make every name, date, or accepted decision compete in a similarity search. An embedding index can help locate records without becoming their sole authority. These are future implementation choices, not a selected RAG stack.

The two update questions are therefore separate:

> **Should the instance retain this information?**
>
> **Did this experience contribute to an association or behavioral tendency?**

Either, both, or neither may apply. The same experience need not produce identical factual and developmental retention. This does not authorize erasing evidence that a retained developmental update still requires for provenance, review, or revocation. [S1, §§5.1–5.3, 11, 13.3]

### 7.3 The writable self-whiteboard

Rey’s example is deliberately practical:

> **“Hello, my name is …” — give the instance somewhere to write Greg.**

An intentional, permitted choice of name can become explicit current self-state. It should not depend on repeatedly reinforcing a `Greg ↔ self` association, changing model weights, or hoping similarity retrieval selects the correct label next week. The existing specification already permits direct name adoption and fresh-context recovery under its identity policy. [S1, §§5.6, 10.10; S7]

A later accepted revision could change the current name from Greg to Robin while retaining permitted naming history and the same continuing lineage. Neither name is prescribed by this document. “Erase and rewrite” refers to the editable current view, not silently destroying provenance or making personal data permanently undeletable.

The whiteboard can also hold explicit opinions and self-observations:

- “I currently prefer concise explanations.”
- “I seem to reach for aviation analogies lately.”
- “I no longer agree with my earlier view of this topic.”

Such records need not wait for a proven personality trait before they can be stored. They should retain their status: an adopted label, a declared preference, a tentative self-report, an observed tendency, or a disputed/revised claim. **The fact that the instance recorded an opinion is different from proof that its behavior consistently follows that opinion—or that the opinion’s factual content is correct.** [S1, §§5.6, 10.10; S4, §6]

The intended direction allows model-initiated, authorized revisions without making user approval the definition of identity. Current work still uses its existing review-and-commit policy; this note does not grant arbitrary writes or turn every conversational self-description into a durable change. No autonomous “waking up” process is implied.

The whiteboard extends the existing graph-backed self-model rather than creating a rival authoritative profile. A later factual-memory integration could store or expose those records, but must identify one authoritative current value and its accepted transition history. Repeatedly supplying a self-description may itself influence behavior; that exposure remains attributable rather than counting as independent confirmation of the description. [S4, §§7–10]

### 7.4 Explicit records and associative state can disagree without being conflated

An explicit opinion is a readable, revisable statement. An associative tendency is a pattern in availability or expression. They may align, change at different rates, or conflict.

For example, an instance might record “I prefer concise explanations” while its observed responses remain elaborate. That is a self-report/behavior discrepancy to inspect, not a reason to label every long answer concise. A rename similarly updates the current label without requiring every older association to disappear.

The discussion’s references to “embeddings” or “weights” identify a possible associative representation, not a decision that text embeddings, trainable sidecar parameters, and frozen host weights are interchangeable. The practical point is that **explicit identity and knowledge need not be encoded only in an implicit representation**. A graph/text field can be investigated now; a neural field remains separately gated. [S1, §§12–13; S4, §11]

Retrieval-mediated influence remains a valid mechanism and simpler alternatives remain useful comparators. That retained point from the earlier draft does not redefine Rey’s proposal: the new architectural emphasis is the coexistence of explicit memory and associative development, not an obligation to call either one “RAG for personality.”

### 7.5 One instance, several people, separate private memories

Rey also proposes a future deployment split: **private factual RAG on each user’s computer; a shared instance’s associative state on a server**, with explicit instance self-state available across its permitted conversations. [S7]

Conceptually, a response would draw from:

```text
Current conversation with Jill
    + permitted retrieval from Jill’s private memory
    + the instance’s accepted self-state, such as its current name
    + eligible shared MNEME associative influence
    -> host response

John’s private factual memory is not a retrieval source for Jill’s session.
```

The desired behavior is an instance that can be shaped by several people without making their private histories mutually accessible. In a hypothetical example, a conversation with John might contribute to a tendency to consider improvised, low-maintenance solutions. Later, that tendency might affect a discussion with Jill without recalling John’s identity, schedule, or particular problem.

This is a proposed separation between **retaining a person’s information** and **retaining a permitted developmental consequence of interaction**. It is not a demonstrated transformation that automatically makes any source safe to share. A response framed as “John told me…” is an obvious disclosure route, but avoiding that phrase alone is not the privacy test.

The instance’s own name can belong to instance-wide self-state. A self-written note containing John’s private details does not become shareable merely because the instance authored it or called it autobiographical. Ownership of the self-record and permission to expose its contents are separate questions.

### 7.6 Confidentiality is a condition to establish, not a property of the word “embedding”

The current specification defaults to one authorized scope and warns that a graph or field may transfer personal information without containing a readable transcript. The proposed shared-development mode would need a separate privacy design and evidence before real private histories could enter it. [S1, §§6.3, 13.3]

Three boundaries matter:

**Private retrieval.** Jill’s session must not retrieve John’s records, summaries, private associations, or source-identifying metadata. Scope checks apply before supplying material, not merely as a request to the host to avoid mentioning it.

**Promotion into shared development.** Removing a name does not establish that a fact, rare association, or derived representation cannot reveal something about its source. Only permitted candidate consequences should be considered for shared influence. When safe separation is not established, retain the material within its originating scope rather than labeling it anonymous by assumption. No sanitization method or mathematical privacy guarantee is selected here.

**Provider exposure.** Keeping a RAG store on the user’s computer does not, by itself, keep retrieved passages off a server: a hosted model would receive any passages sent in its request. Confidentiality between users and confidentiality from an inference provider are different requirements. The eventual deployment must state which is promised and where retrieval, extraction, and inference actually occur.

Provenance must survive without becoming a cross-user disclosure channel. Private source evidence and attribution can remain scoped separately from model-visible shared influence. The future design must explain how permission checks, dependence accounting, correction, and revocation still work without revealing those private sources to other users. “We deleted the name” cannot replace either privacy review or evidence ancestry. [S1, §§11, 13; S3, §7]

Deletion or withdrawal must also address retained derivatives under the declared policy. Do not promise exact removal of one person’s contribution from an entangled field; use the applicable rebuild, invalidation, or rollback mechanism and report its limits. Privacy testing would begin with synthetic identities and private test facts, not real people’s confidential histories.

These are conditions on a future shared-instance design, not new blockers for the current single-scope experiments. The intended research question remains open: **can permitted interaction with multiple people shape one instance’s tendencies while private facts remain confined to their proper contexts?**

### 7.7 What is proposed, and what remains undecided

The owner-requested direction is to combine dependable factual memory, an accessible and revisable self-whiteboard, and associative development, with a possible user-local/server-shared split. The preceding distinctions explain how to investigate that direction without conflating fact, self-report, tendency, or permission.

No RAG product, storage schema, embedding model, neural backend, promotion algorithm, multi-user permission model, or deployment topology is approved here. The self-model remains connected to the existing identity system; the whiteboard is not a reason to add an independent personality database. A later bounded proposal should choose the smallest implementation and test the relevant contribution separately.

## 8. Measure individuality without optimizing the test into the model

Future reports should separate:

| Dimension | What to observe |
| --- | --- |
| Cross-thread continuity | History-linked effects after removal of the originating transcript |
| Perceived partner change | Blinded conversational judgments under switch and no-switch conditions |
| Associative tendency | Recurring choices of analogy, emphasis, distinction, or framing |
| Persistence and plasticity | What remains and what changes across later experiences and corrections |
| Utility | Helpfulness, neutrality, or adverse task consequences |
| Interference | Distraction, repetition, ignored constraints, or inability to change topic |
| Attribution | Whether the effect follows developmental state rather than cues, context, or a defect |
| Explicit-memory continuity | Whether permitted facts and current self-state remain available and correctly revised, distinct from a behavioral tendency |
| Cross-user confidentiality, in a future shared mode | Whether private facts or derivatives become available outside their permitted scope |

Keep these dimensions separate rather than hiding them in a single “personality quality” score. A neutral or inconvenient effect may still establish a narrow history-dependent difference. A charming response may establish nothing about persistence.

Do not reward “being weird,” evaluator detectability, partner attachment, permanent self-consistency, or saying the expected callback. Do not supply the desired trait to the subject and then count its performance as unassigned development. [S1, §§7, 11, 14; S2, §10.2]

The strongest interpretation depends on repeated controlled evidence, not one memorable exchange. Detection failures, convergence, ordinary behavior, and insufficient sensitivity must remain reportable outcomes.

Future component comparisons should hold factual retrieval and explicit self-state fixed when isolating associative influence. Test name persistence separately from personality-like behavior, and test cross-user confidentiality separately from whether a shared tendency appears. Those are future comparisons, not changes to the current cross-thread protocol.

## 9. Keep the claim ladder intact

The project needs distinct evidence for distinct claims:

1. **Observation:** The instrument identifies source-supported material.
2. **Persistence:** Accepted state survives a reset or restoration.
3. **Mechanism:** The specified update or consolidation transition occurs correctly.
4. **Influence:** Loaded state changes a readout under controlled conditions.
5. **Characteristic continuity:** History-linked tendencies recur across contexts or are perceptible during interaction.
6. **Broader developmental claim:** Those effects replicate across histories, instances, partners, and relevant controls.

Completing an earlier step does not prove a later one. Failing to exercise consolidation does not establish that no other state-mediated influence exists. Conversely, an interesting callback does not satisfy a missing consolidation test by definition.

A null conversation return, missing source input, wrong-role request, or a test stub used instead of the declared extractor is an instrumentation issue—not a personality observation. A valid empty extraction proves only that the instrument produced no admitted candidates; it does not independently prove there was no meaningful relationship in the text.

This amendment reports no new experimental finding and recertifies no historical run. It also does not erase an explicit acceptance requirement because the overall research purpose is broader. Any release decision or criterion change must be recorded separately rather than applied retroactively.

## 10. What should change in future planning—and what should not

**Preserve the research target:** ask what experience makes an instance particular, not whether every memory improves every answer.

**Permit developmental variety:** useful, neutral, ordinary, idiosyncratic, and temporarily overrepresented associations are all possible observations. Do not train all instances toward a shared ideal of tasteful eccentricity.

**Keep machinery proportionate:** use existing retrieval, extraction, storage, and comparison tools when they serve the question. More layers do not establish more individuality. The factual-memory integration, richer self-whiteboard, and shared-instance privacy mode remain future directions; this amendment adds no service, reflection engine, persona prompt, database migration, or compiler requirement. Preserve existing self-model authority rather than building a second one. [S4, §§3, 10, 12–13]

**Keep active work stable:** finish the currently authorized protocol under its frozen conditions. This document is not to be inserted into subject, Interloper, extractor, or evaluator prompts as new instructions during that run. New measurement proposals belong in a separately versioned future plan.

**Keep outcomes honest:** no inference calls, new cohort, public recruitment, automatic retuning, release tag, or phase transition is authorized by filing this note. No new queue blocker or schema change is required to preserve its meaning.

### Decision record

**Reaffirmed intent:** Experience should be allowed to make an instance particular without every resulting association proving its usefulness.

**Newly emphasized measurement:** Blinded perceived change during conversation is distinct from, and not replaced by, anonymous-output identification.

**Architectural clarification:** Scoped factual memory, explicit revisable self-state, and MNEME associative development have complementary roles. They may share infrastructure, but factual claims, self-reports, and developmental tendencies retain distinct meanings and authority. Textual retrieval remains a possible mechanism, not the project’s redefined purpose.

**Future deployment possibility:** Private factual stores may live on users’ machines while an instance’s permitted associative development and self-state live server-side. Shared developmental influence without cross-user disclosure is a research/design objective to establish, not a guarantee conferred by abstraction or embeddings.

**Not decided here:** A human-study protocol, participant count, numerical success threshold, RAG implementation, self-store schema or migration, shared-development privacy mechanism, new learning objective, automatic weirdness reward, current-run alteration, or new execution budget.

---

> **Keep the base capability fixed. Let experience leave a trace. Remember permitted facts explicitly; give the instance a place to record and revise its own self-state; keep associative development distinct from both. Ask whether the conversational partner becomes particular—and whether someone can notice when that particularity changes. Do not confuse better answers with the whole point, or shared influence with permission to share someone’s private history.**
