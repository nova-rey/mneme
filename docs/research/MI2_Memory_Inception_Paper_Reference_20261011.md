## Procedural bank examples and paper evidence

Appendix E Tables 4–7 (printed pp. 18–21; PDF pp. 19–22) show that a reasoning bank is a compact process card with a trigger/key idea, an action, and a trap/check—not a solved instance. Examples, paraphrased:

- **Quantum mechanics:** identify whether the task concerns spectra, time evolution, measurement, perturbative response, or asymptotics; choose a representation suited to that question; keep amplitudes, phases, probabilities, and observables distinct.
- **Electromagnetism:** establish interface/boundary and constitutive conditions before solving each region’s bulk equations; verify the fields satisfy interface behavior.
- **HARDMath integrals:** identify the parameter and convergence domain, choose among substitution, differentiation under the integral, symmetry/contour reduction, or known-transform matching; check the result by limits, differentiation, or a numeric substitution.
- **HARDMath ODEs:** identify homogeneous/particular structure and singular/conserved features before applying boundary values; substitute the final solution into the equation and boundary conditions.

Short source excerpts (total 11 words by whitespace tokenization): “process-only heuristic cards” (Appendix D.4, printed p. 17 / PDF p. 18); “Construction examples are excluded from the evaluation set” (§4, printed p. 6 / PDF p. 7). These passages anchor the intended bank/evaluation separation.

Relevant outcome context: Table 1 (§5, printed p. 6 / PDF p. 7) reports PHYSICS Qwen3 overall averages of 73.9% plain, 75.4% visible prompt, 76.9% MI; MI wins 10 of 12 subject×mode cells. HARDMath CoT overall is 60.8% plain, 67.3% prompt, 63.8% MI. Appendix F.5 says heuristic banks are especially useful when the benchmark rewards reusable subject-level procedures; F.6 describes HARDMath as more mixed. These are author-reported results on Qwen3, not predictions for Gemma.

## Evidence classification and limits

- **Observed:** the paper’s scientific guidance result is process-prior use on held-out PHYSICS/HARDMath questions; MI1’s primary fictional chain-retrieval task differs. MI1’s later context wrappers and selected-site machinery partially follow the paper’s technical recipe, while its Gemma runtime remains a custom port.
- **Inferred:** the authorized MI2 design is a materially closer test of the paper’s strongest structured-reasoning claim if it uses source-derived process guidance, clean construction/evaluation separation, visible positive controls, irrelevant/no-bank controls, and a frozen Gemma selector.
- **Unresolved:** the paper does not qualify Gemma Q2/llama.cpp; whether MI1’s custom Gemma K/V/attention implementation matches paper semantics remains unknown. The exact anonymous author release is not established, and Appendix C gives the clearest selector details for dense Llama questionnaire runs rather than a complete structured-reasoning selector recipe.

The paper itself cautions that bank quality is task-dependent, selector calibration is required, reported effects vary by backbone, and KV accounting is storage-equivalent rather than realized VRAM/latency (§7, printed p. 9 / PDF p. 10). MI2 should retain that scope.
